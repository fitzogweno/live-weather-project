Nairobi Produce Market Analysis
1. Executive Summary

This project analyses simulated daily prices for five vegetables in Nairobi over a six-month period, January to June 2026.

The dataset contains 905 observations covering tomatoes, onions, potatoes, cabbages and carrots.

The objective was to identify the vegetable with the highest price volatility and determine when its volatility peaks.

2. Key Findings
Tomatoes recorded the highest daily price volatility at 12.76%.
Cabbages followed at 9.40%.
Carrots recorded 7.53%.
Onions recorded 6.65%.
Potatoes were the most stable at 5.09%.
3. Peak Volatility

Tomatoes experienced their highest monthly volatility in May 2026, reaching 14.06%.

This indicates that daily tomato price changes were particularly unpredictable during this month.

4. Business Implications
Tomato traders face greater short-term price uncertainty.
Traders should monitor tomato prices more frequently.
Flexible purchasing and pricing strategies can help manage price risk.
Potatoes may offer more predictable short-term pricing.
5. Recommendation

Market traders should pay particular attention to tomato prices during May and use frequent price monitoring to inform purchasing and selling decisions.

6. Methodology

Python, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib and Seaborn were used.

Daily price volatility was calculated using the standard deviation of daily percentage price changes.

Monthly volatility was calculated separately for each vegetable using the standard deviation of daily percentage returns within each month.

7. Data Limitations

The dataset was synthetically generated for educational purposes. It does not represent verified historical Nairobi market prices.

The findings should therefore be treated as an analytical demonstration rather than actual market evidence.

8. Conclusion

Tomatoes were the most volatile vegetable in the simulated dataset, with May 2026 recording the highest monthly volatility.

The analysis demonstrates how data analytics can help market participants understand price fluctuations and make more informed decisions.
